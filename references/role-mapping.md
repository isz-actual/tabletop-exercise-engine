## ICS-to-Corporate Role Mapping

The Incident Command System provides a scalable organizational structure. In a corporate setting, ICS positions map to existing corporate roles. The table below shows the standard mapping. Customize based on the user's organizational structure.

### Command Staff

| ICS Position | ICS Function | Corporate Equivalent(s) | Exercise Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Incident Commander | Overall incident authority, sets objectives, approves IAP | CSO, VP Security, Director of Security, or designated Crisis Manager | In corporate TTX, this is typically the senior security leader unless the scenario escalates to CEO/COO authority |
| Deputy Incident Commander | Supports IC, assumes command if IC unavailable | Assistant Director of Security, Security Operations Manager | Important to exercise succession planning |
| Public Information Officer (PIO) | Media relations, public messaging, internal comms | VP Communications, PR Director, Corporate Communications Manager | Critical role for any scenario with media exposure |
| Safety Officer | Monitors safety conditions, has authority to stop unsafe actions | EHS Director, Safety Manager, Risk Manager | Important for scenarios involving physical hazards |
| Liaison Officer | Point of contact for assisting agencies | Government Affairs, External Relations, Legal | Critical for scenarios involving law enforcement or regulators |

### General Staff (Section Chiefs)

| ICS Position | ICS Function | Corporate Equivalent(s) | Exercise Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Operations Section Chief | Directs tactical response activities | Security Operations Manager, Site Security Director | Manages the "doing" during the incident |
| Planning Section Chief | Collects/evaluates information, maintains status, develops IAP | Business Continuity Manager, Emergency Management Coordinator, Intelligence Analyst | Manages the "thinking" and documentation |
| Logistics Section Chief | Provides resources, services, and support | Facilities Director, Office Manager, Procurement | Manages the "getting" of resources |
| Finance/Admin Section Chief | Tracks costs, procurement, compensation, claims | Finance Director, Controller, CFO delegate | Often overlooked in exercises but critical for insurance, cost tracking |

### Branch/Division Level (for larger incidents)

| ICS Position | Corporate Equivalent(s) |
|---|---|
| Operations Branch Director | Regional Security Manager |
| Situation Unit Leader | Threat Intelligence Analyst |
| Resources Unit Leader | HR Operations Manager |
| Communications Unit Leader | IT/Telecom Manager |
| Medical Unit Leader | Occupational Health Manager |
| Documentation Unit Leader | Legal/Compliance Coordinator |

## Exercise Staff Roles

These are people who RUN the exercise, not people who PLAY in it.

### Exercise Director
**Who:** Senior leader who authorizes and oversees the exercise.
**Responsibilities:**
- Approves exercise design and objectives
- Ensures organizational support and participation
- May deliver opening/closing remarks
- Does NOT facilitate discussions (delegates to Lead Facilitator)

### Lead Facilitator
**Who:** The person running the exercise minute-to-minute.
**Responsibilities:**
- Delivers scenario narration
- Asks discussion questions
- Manages time and pacing
- Calls on participants, manages group dynamics
- Delivers or cues inject delivery
- Redirects off-topic discussion
- Runs the hot wash debrief
**Qualifications:** Should be comfortable speaking to senior leadership, familiar with the scenario domain, and experienced in group facilitation. Can be internal (security team member) or external (consultant).

### Controller(s)
**Who:** Exercise staff who deliver injects and keep exercise on track.
**Responsibilities:**
- Deliver assigned injects at designated times via designated methods
- Monitor exercise pace and flag timing issues to facilitator
- Deploy contingency injects when directed
- Do NOT coach players or suggest answers
- SimCell operators are a type of controller
**Number needed:** 1-2 for small TTX (under 20 players), 2-4 for large TTX (20+ players), additional if SimCell is used.

### Evaluator(s)
**Who:** Observers who document player actions and decisions against exercise objectives.
**Responsibilities:**
- Observe and document player actions, decisions, and discussions
- Note strengths and areas for improvement
- Complete evaluator worksheets during exercise
- Contribute findings to AAR/IP development
- Do NOT participate in discussion or influence player decisions
**Number needed:** Minimum 1 per objective being evaluated. Ideal: 1 per functional area represented.

### Data Collector (optional)
**Who:** Support staff who records exercise details.
**Responsibilities:**
- Records timeline of events and decisions
- Captures flip chart notes, whiteboard content
- Photographs room setup and artifacts created during exercise
- Maintains exercise documentation

## Player Role Descriptions

Each player should receive a role card that includes:

### Role Card Template:
```
EXERCISE ROLE CARD

Name: [Participant Name]
Exercise Role: [ICS Position Title]
Corporate Title: [Actual or simulated corporate title]
Department: [Department name]

Your Responsibilities During This Exercise:
- [2-3 bullet points describing what this role is responsible for during the scenario]

Key Decisions You May Need to Make:
- [1-2 examples of the types of decisions this role faces]

Who You Should Coordinate With:
- [List of other exercise roles this person needs to communicate with]

Reference Materials:
- [Relevant plan sections, procedures, contact lists]
```

## Scaling Roles by Exercise Size

### Small TTX (8-12 players)
Combine roles. One person may cover:
- IC + Operations Chief
- PIO + Liaison
- Planning + Logistics
- Finance may be observer only
- 1 Facilitator, 1 Controller/Evaluator

### Medium TTX (15-25 players)
Full ICS Command and General Staff, plus:
- 2-3 department representatives (HR, Legal, IT)
- 1-2 executive observers or players
- 1 Facilitator, 2 Controllers, 2 Evaluators

### Large TTX (25-50 players)
Full ICS structure with branch-level positions, plus:
- Multiple department representatives
- C-suite participation
- SimCell with 3-5 operators
- 1 Lead Facilitator + 1 Assistant Facilitator
- 3-4 Controllers, 3-4 Evaluators
- 1 Data Collector

## C-Suite Integration Guidance

### When to Include C-Suite:
- Scenario involves reputational risk, regulatory exposure, or significant financial impact
- Decision authority exceeds Director/VP level (e.g., ransom payment, public statement, facility closure)
- Board notification is part of the exercise
- Organization wants to demonstrate preparedness to board or regulators

### How to Integrate C-Suite Effectively:
1. **Brief them separately** before the exercise on format and expectations
2. **Assign them specific decision points** rather than having them observe the full exercise
3. **Bring them in at Module 3** when the scenario has escalated to their level
4. **Give them realistic constraints** (incomplete information, time pressure, competing advice from staff)
5. **Frame their participation as a discussion**, not a test of their knowledge
6. **Protect their time** by having them participate for 30-45 min of a longer exercise

### C-Suite Decision Points to Build Into Scenarios:
- Authorize ransom payment (yes/no/negotiate)
- Approve public statement or press conference
- Order facility closure or evacuation
- Authorize termination or law enforcement referral
- Approve emergency expenditure above normal authority
- Decide on regulatory self-disclosure
- Authorize board notification
- Approve business continuity plan activation
