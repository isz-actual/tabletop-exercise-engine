## Exercise Overview Document

One-page document for leadership approval and exercise authorization. Include:

**Header Block:**
- Exercise Name (descriptive, not classified scenario details)
- Exercise Type: Tabletop Exercise (TTX)
- Date and Time
- Location
- Classification/Sensitivity Marking
- Exercise Director name and contact
- Point of Contact name and contact

**Body:**
- Purpose: 2-3 sentences on why this exercise is being conducted
- Scope: What is being tested (and explicitly, what is NOT being tested)
- Objectives: Numbered list of 2-4 measurable objectives, each starting with an action verb (Assess, Validate, Evaluate, Examine, Test)
- Scenario Summary: 1 paragraph, high-level, no sensitive details
- Participants: List of organizations/departments and approximate headcount
- Exercise Structure: Duration, number of modules, format (in-person/virtual/hybrid)
- Assumptions and Artificialities: Key simplifications
- Administrative Notes: Parking, food, security clearance requirements, NDAs

## Situation Manual Structure

The SitMan is the primary player document. It should be professional, branded if possible, and structured as follows:

### Cover Page
- Exercise name
- Date
- "EXERCISE - FOR OFFICIAL USE ONLY" marking
- "This document contains exercise information. It is not to be used for any other purpose."
- Organization logo placeholder [USER INPUT: provide logo or leave placeholder]

### Handling Instructions (Page 2)
"The [Exercise Name] Situation Manual (SitMan) is intended for use by exercise participants only. This document contains information that may be considered sensitive. The material in this document should be treated as if it were classified and handled accordingly. The SitMan should not be distributed to non-participants or released publicly."

### Table of Contents

### Section 1: Exercise Overview
- Exercise name, date, scope, type
- Mission areas being exercised
- Core capabilities being exercised (align to FEMA 32 core capabilities where applicable)
- Exercise objectives (verbatim from overview document)
- Exercise structure and agenda
- Exercise rules and ground rules
- Exercise assumptions and artificialities

### Section 2: General Information
- Participant roles and responsibilities
- Exercise facilitators, controllers, evaluators
- Exercise communication and logistics
- Safety and security during exercise

### Section 3: Scenario Background
Build the scenario in layers:

**3.1 General Conditions** (organizational context)
- Company profile (use actual or sanitized version)
- Current threat environment
- Season/weather/time of day
- Operational tempo (normal business day, special event, holiday)

**3.2 Pre-Incident Intelligence / Indicators** (optional, for scenarios with lead-up)
- Threat indicators observed in days/weeks prior
- Relevant BOLO, intelligence bulletins, HR reports
- System alerts, access anomalies, behavioral observations

**3.3 Scenario Narrative**
- The initiating event described in narrative form
- Written in present tense
- Includes enough detail for players to begin making decisions
- Does NOT include resolution or predetermined outcomes

### Section 4: Exercise Modules

Each module represents a phase of the incident. Typical structure:

**Module 1: Initial Response / Detection** (20-30 min)
- Scenario update (new information delivered via inject or narration)
- Discussion questions (5-7 questions)

**Module 2: Escalation / Assessment** (20-30 min)
- Scenario update showing escalation or complication
- Discussion questions requiring cross-functional coordination

**Module 3: Crisis Management / Decision Point** (20-30 min)
- Scenario update with C-suite decision requirement
- Discussion questions on leadership authority, external coordination

**Module 4: Resolution / Recovery** (15-20 min)
- Scenario update moving toward resolution
- Discussion questions on transition to recovery, communications, lessons learned

### Section 5: Appendices
- Organizational chart (exercise-specific)
- Relevant plan excerpts (EAP, Crisis Management Plan, BCP)
- Maps/floor plans (if relevant to scenario)
- Contact lists (exercise-specific, not real numbers)
- Acronym list

## Facilitator Guide Structure

The Facilitator Guide is the exercise director's playbook. It contains everything in the SitMan PLUS:

### Additional Facilitator-Only Content:

**Pre-Exercise Checklist:**
- 8 weeks out: Confirm date, secure venue, identify planning team
- 6 weeks out: Finalize objectives and scenario concept
- 4 weeks out: Complete SitMan draft, identify participants, send invitations
- 2 weeks out: Finalize all documents, brief controllers/evaluators
- 1 week out: Confirm logistics, print materials, test A/V
- Day of: Arrive 90 min early, set room, brief C/E team

**Facilitator Notes per Module:**
For each module, include:
- Key teaching points to draw out
- Expected player responses (what good looks like)
- Common mistakes or gaps to watch for
- Redirect prompts if discussion stalls
- Time management cues
- Inject delivery instructions (who delivers, how, exact timing)

**Contingency Injects:**
Pre-written injects for when:
- Players solve the problem too quickly (add complication)
- Players get stuck (provide additional information)
- Discussion goes off-topic (redirect inject)
- Key player role is absent (adjust scenario to account for gap)

**Hot Wash Facilitation Guide:**
- Opening statement script
- Structured debrief questions:
  1. What went well?
  2. What were the most significant challenges?
  3. Were our plans adequate for this scenario?
  4. What would you do differently?
  5. What are the top 3 corrective actions we should prioritize?
- Closing statement script
- Participant feedback form distribution instructions

## Ground Rules and Artificialities

### Standard Ground Rules (include in every SitMan):

1. This is an exercise. No real-world actions should be taken based on exercise play.
2. There are no wrong answers. The exercise is designed to generate discussion, not test individuals.
3. All discussions during this exercise are covered by the Chatham House Rule. Observations may be referenced in the AAR, but individual comments will not be attributed.
4. Participants should respond as they would in a real incident, using existing plans and procedures.
5. Cell phones should be silenced. If you must take a call, please step out.
6. The facilitator will manage time. When asked to move on, please do so.
7. All participants are encouraged to speak. If you have not contributed to a module, the facilitator may call on you.
8. Do not share exercise materials outside this room without authorization.

### Standard Artificialities:

1. The exercise timeline is compressed. Events that would unfold over hours or days may be presented in minutes.
2. Not all organizational functions are represented. Some responses that would normally occur in parallel are addressed sequentially.
3. The scenario is designed to stress the response system. Some conditions may be more challenging than a typical incident.
4. Participants may need to assume capabilities or resources that are not physically present.
5. External entities (law enforcement, regulators, media) are simulated by exercise staff.

## Scenario Development Methodology

Follow the HSEEP "right-to-left" planning approach:

1. **Start with objectives** -- what do you want to test?
2. **Identify capabilities** needed to meet those objectives
3. **Design the scenario** that will force those capabilities to be demonstrated
4. **Build injects** that create decision points aligned to capabilities
5. **Write discussion questions** that draw out whether capabilities were demonstrated

### Scenario Realism Checklist:
- [ ] Scenario is plausible for this organization and industry
- [ ] Timeline is realistic (even if compressed)
- [ ] Information provided to players is what they would realistically have
- [ ] Ambiguity exists (players do not have perfect information)
- [ ] Multiple valid courses of action exist (no single "right answer")
- [ ] Scenario escalates in complexity across modules
- [ ] At least one inject creates cross-functional tension or competing priorities
- [ ] External pressures are represented (media, regulators, families, public)

## Module Design Pattern

Each module follows this structure:

```
MODULE [#]: [TITLE]
Duration: [XX] minutes
Objective(s) Tested: [list]

[SCENARIO UPDATE]
Narrative text delivered by facilitator or via inject slide.
Include: what has changed, what new information is available,
what the current situation looks like.

[KEY INFORMATION]
Any data, reports, or artifacts provided to players.
(e.g., initial damage report, threat assessment summary,
social media screenshot, news alert text)

[DISCUSSION QUESTIONS]
1. [Question targeting specific capability]
2. [Question targeting specific capability]
3. [Question requiring cross-functional input]
4. [Question targeting leadership decision authority]
5. [Question targeting communications/external coordination]

[FACILITATOR NOTES - FACILITATOR GUIDE ONLY]
- Expected responses
- Gaps to probe
- Redirect prompts
- Time management notes
```

## Discussion Questions Framework

Good discussion questions follow these principles:

**Do:**
- Start with action verbs: "What would you do...", "How would you...", "Who has authority to..."
- Target specific roles: "As the HR lead, what is your immediate concern?"
- Force prioritization: "You have two competing demands. Which takes priority?"
- Test handoffs: "At what point does this escalate from Security to Legal?"
- Challenge assumptions: "What if [key system/person/resource] is unavailable?"
- Probe communications: "What message goes to employees? When? Through what channel?"

**Avoid:**
- Yes/no questions
- Questions with obvious answers
- Questions that only one person can answer
- Questions that require specialized knowledge not held by participants
- Leading questions that telegraph the "right" answer

### Question Categories:

**Notification and Activation:**
Who gets notified first? What triggers activation of the crisis management team? What is the communication chain?

**Decision Authority:**
Who has authority to make this decision? What happens if that person is unavailable? What is the approval threshold?

**Coordination:**
Which departments need to be involved? What information do they need from each other? Where are the handoff points?

**Communications:**
What do we tell employees? When? What do we tell the public? Who speaks to media? What do we tell regulators?

**Resource Allocation:**
What resources are needed? Where do they come from? What competing demands exist?

**Legal and Regulatory:**
What are our legal obligations? What notifications are required? What documentation must we preserve?

**Recovery and Continuity:**
When do we transition from response to recovery? What does return to normal operations look like? What long-term actions are needed?

## Logistics and Setup

### Room Configuration:
- U-shape or boardroom style preferred for discussion-based exercises
- Projector/screen for scenario slides and injects
- Name tents with player role and department
- Printed SitMan for each player
- Note paper and pens
- Clock visible to facilitator
- Separate table or breakout space for C/E team if needed

### Technology Requirements (Virtual/Hybrid):
- Video conferencing platform with screen share
- Digital SitMan distributed in advance (marked "Do Not Open Until Directed")
- Chat function for inject delivery
- Breakout rooms capability for small-group discussion
- Recording capability (with consent) for evaluator review

### Materials Checklist:
- [ ] Situation Manuals (printed or digital)
- [ ] Facilitator Guide
- [ ] MSEL (for facilitator and controllers only)
- [ ] Evaluator Worksheets
- [ ] Name tents and sign-in sheet
- [ ] Participant feedback forms
- [ ] Exercise slides (scenario updates, injects)
- [ ] Organization charts and maps (as appendices or wall display)
- [ ] "THIS IS AN EXERCISE" signage (if in shared building)
