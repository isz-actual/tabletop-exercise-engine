## Evaluation Methodology

Per HSEEP doctrine, exercise evaluation follows this process:

1. **Before the exercise:** Evaluators receive evaluator worksheets aligned to exercise objectives and assigned functional areas
2. **During the exercise:** Evaluators observe, listen, and document player actions, decisions, and discussions without participating
3. **Immediately after:** Evaluators participate in a C/E debrief to share initial observations
4. **Hot wash:** Facilitator leads player debrief to capture player perspectives
5. **Within 2 weeks:** Lead evaluator drafts AAR/IP using evaluator notes, hot wash notes, and participant feedback
6. **Within 30-60 days:** AAR/IP is finalized, distributed, and improvement plan tracking begins

### Evaluation Focus Areas

For each exercise objective, evaluators assess:
- **What happened?** Factual description of actions taken (or not taken)
- **What was the consequence?** Positive or negative impact of the action
- **Why did it happen?** Root cause analysis of both strengths and gaps
- **What should change?** Recommended corrective action (for gaps) or sustained practice (for strengths)

## Hot Wash Facilitation

The hot wash is the immediate post-exercise debrief with all players. It should be conducted within 15-20 minutes of exercise conclusion.

### Hot Wash Script:

**Opening:**
"Thank you all for your participation in [Exercise Name]. Before we leave today, I want to capture your initial thoughts while they are fresh. This is an informal discussion. I will ask a few structured questions, but please also share anything that stood out to you. Remember, the Chatham House Rule applies -- I will capture themes and observations for the after-action report, but individual comments will not be attributed."

**Structured Questions:**
1. "Starting with what went well -- what processes, plans, or capabilities performed as expected or better?"
2. "What were the most significant challenges you encountered during the exercise? Where did you feel uncertain or unprepared?"
3. "Were there any surprises? Anything you did not expect?"
4. "Did you have the information you needed to make decisions? If not, what was missing?"
5. "Were there gaps in coordination between departments or functions? Where were the handoff points that need improvement?"
6. "If this were a real incident, what would you do differently knowing what you know now?"
7. "What are the top three actions we should take as a result of this exercise?"

**Closing:**
"Thank you. Your feedback is invaluable and will inform the after-action report. The AAR/IP will be distributed within [timeframe]. Please also complete the participant feedback form before you leave. This exercise was conducted under [classification/sensitivity marking]. Please do not discuss specific scenario details outside this group. Thank you for your time and engagement."

### Hot Wash Documentation:
Assign one person (Data Collector or Evaluator) to capture hot wash comments on flip chart, whiteboard, or laptop. Capture themes, not verbatim quotes. Group responses by: Strengths, Areas for Improvement, Top Corrective Actions.

## Participant Feedback Form

Distribute to all players at exercise conclusion. Can be paper or digital.

### Feedback Form Content:

**Part 1: Exercise Rating (1-5 scale, where 1=Strongly Disagree, 5=Strongly Agree)**
1. The exercise objectives were clearly stated.
2. The scenario was realistic and relevant to our organization.
3. The exercise was well organized.
4. The facilitator(s) effectively guided the discussion.
5. The exercise improved my understanding of my role during a crisis.
6. The exercise identified areas where our plans or procedures need improvement.
7. I would recommend this type of exercise to colleagues.
8. The exercise was an appropriate length.

**Part 2: Open-Ended Questions**
1. What was the most valuable aspect of this exercise?
2. What is the single most important improvement we should make based on this exercise?
3. How could this exercise be improved for next time?
4. Any additional comments?

**Part 3: Demographics (optional)**
- Department: _______________
- Years with organization: _______________
- Previous exercise experience: [ ] Yes  [ ] No

## AAR/IP Document Structure

The AAR/IP is the formal record of the exercise and the primary vehicle for driving improvement. Follow HSEEP format.

### Document Outline:

**Cover Page**
- Organization name and logo
- Exercise name
- "After-Action Report / Improvement Plan"
- Date of exercise
- Date of report
- "EXERCISE - FOR OFFICIAL USE ONLY"
- Point of contact

**Administrative Handling Instructions**
Statement that this document is for internal use, exercise-derived information should not be confused with real-world events, and distribution is limited.

**Table of Contents**

**Chapter 1: Exercise Overview**
Write in paragraph form:
- Exercise Name
- Exercise Type (Tabletop Exercise)
- Exercise Date and Duration
- Mission Area(s) (Prevention, Protection, Mitigation, Response, Recovery)
- Core Capabilities Exercised
- Objectives
- Threat/Hazard (scenario type)
- Scenario Summary (2-3 paragraphs, unclassified)
- Participating Organizations (list departments)
- Number of Participants
- Exercise Planning Team and Point of Contact

**Chapter 2: Exercise Design Summary**
Brief description of exercise structure, number of modules, types of injects, key decision points, and how the exercise was designed to meet the stated objectives.

**Chapter 3: Analysis of Capabilities**
This is the most important chapter. Organize by objective or by capability.

For each objective/capability:
1. State the objective
2. List related activities tested
3. Present observations (strengths and areas for improvement)
4. For each observation, include:
   - Observation title (one sentence)
   - Analysis (what happened, why it matters, root cause)
   - References (plans, procedures, or standards that apply)

**Chapter 4: Conclusion**
Summarize overall exercise findings. State that the organization demonstrated [strengths] while identifying opportunities to improve [areas]. Note that the Improvement Plan in Appendix A captures specific corrective actions.

**Appendix A: Improvement Plan**
See "Improvement Plan Development" section below.

**Appendix B: Participant Feedback Summary** (optional)
Aggregate results from feedback forms. Include average ratings and themed open-ended responses.

**Appendix C: Exercise Events Summary** (optional)
Condensed version of the MSEL showing key events and outcomes.

**Appendix D: Acronyms**

## Observation Writing Guide

### Format:
Each observation follows this pattern:

**Strength / Area for Improvement [#]:**
[One-sentence observation statement that clearly describes what was observed]

**Analysis:**
[2-4 sentences explaining what happened, the context, the consequence (positive or negative), and the root cause or contributing factors. For strengths, explain what made the response effective. For areas for improvement, explain the gap without blaming individuals.]

**References:**
[Cite relevant plans, policies, procedures, or standards. E.g., "Crisis Management Plan Section 4.2", "ANSI/ASIS WVPI AA-2020 Section 6.3", "Company Emergency Action Plan"]

### Observation Writing Rules:
- Write in third person ("The crisis management team..." not "You...")
- Describe WHAT happened, not WHO did it (roles, not names)
- Be specific and factual, not vague
- Each observation should be actionable
- Strengths deserve as much detail as areas for improvement
- Reference specific moments in the exercise
- Connect observations to exercise objectives

### Example Strength:
**Strength 1:** The crisis management team activated the notification cascade within 5 minutes of the initial report, reaching all required stakeholders.

**Analysis:** Upon receiving the initial incident report in Module 1, the Incident Commander immediately directed the Operations Section to initiate the notification cascade per the Crisis Management Plan. All required departments were notified within the target 5-minute window. The team demonstrated familiarity with the notification protocol and the communication tools available.

**References:** Crisis Management Plan, Appendix B: Notification Matrix; Company Emergency Notification System SOP.

### Example Area for Improvement:
**Area for Improvement 1:** The crisis management team did not establish a unified message for employee communications, resulting in conflicting information being shared across departments.

**Analysis:** During Module 2, as the scenario escalated, multiple departments began drafting independent communications to their teams without coordinating through the Public Information Officer. The HR representative sent guidance that conflicted with the safety instructions from the Security Operations Manager. This created confusion about the appropriate protective action. The root cause was the absence of a pre-approved message template and unclear authority for approving employee communications during a crisis.

**References:** Crisis Communications Plan (draft); ASIS ORM.1 Section 8.4: Communication.

## Improvement Plan Development

The Improvement Plan (IP) is a table that translates observations into tracked corrective actions.

### IP Table Columns:

| # | Observation | Corrective Action | Capability Element | Primary Responsible Organization | Start Date | Completion Date | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | [Observation title from Chapter 3] | [Specific action to address the gap] | [Planning / Organization / Equipment / Training / Exercise] | [Department responsible] | [Date] | [Target date] | [Not Started / In Progress / Complete] |

### Capability Element Definitions (per HSEEP):
- **Planning:** Update or create plans, policies, procedures, or checklists
- **Organization:** Modify organizational structure, roles, responsibilities, or staffing
- **Equipment:** Acquire, maintain, or replace equipment, technology, or systems
- **Training:** Develop or conduct training programs
- **Exercise:** Plan additional exercises to validate corrective actions

### Corrective Action Writing Rules:
- Start with an action verb (Develop, Update, Create, Train, Acquire, Revise, Establish)
- Be specific and measurable
- Assign one primary responsible organization (not shared)
- Set realistic completion dates
- Prioritize: High (complete within 90 days), Medium (complete within 180 days), Low (complete within 1 year)

## Performance Rating System

Optional per HSEEP. Use when required by organizational policy, regulatory requirement, or exercise program maturity warrants it.

| Rating | Label | Description |
|---|---|---|
| **P** | Performed without Challenges | Activities associated with the capability were performed in a manner that achieved the objective and did not negatively impact other activities. Performance met or exceeded planned outcomes. |
| **S** | Performed with Some Challenges | Activities were performed and achieved the objective. Some challenges were observed but did not significantly impact performance. With minor improvement, capability performance would increase. |
| **M** | Performed with Major Challenges | Activities were performed but with significant challenges. One or more of: negative impact on other activities, additional safety risks, not conducted per applicable plans. Significant improvement needed. |
| **U** | Unable to be Performed | Activities could not be performed in a manner that achieved the objective. The capability was not demonstrated. |

Assign ratings at the capability level, not at the individual level.

## Corrective Action Tracking

### Post-AAR/IP Process:
1. AAR/IP distributed to all exercise participants and responsible organizations
2. Executive sponsor reviews and approves improvement plan
3. Each corrective action assigned to a named individual (not just a department)
4. Quarterly progress review meetings
5. Status updates recorded in improvement plan tracking document
6. Corrective actions validated through subsequent exercises, drills, or plan reviews
7. Rolling summary of outcomes maintained per HSEEP progressive planning

### Tracking Spreadsheet Columns:
| IP # | Corrective Action | Priority | Responsible Org | POC Name | Start | Target Completion | Actual Completion | Status | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

## Timeline and Distribution

| Milestone | Timeline | Responsible |
|---|---|---|
| Hot wash conducted | Day of exercise | Lead Facilitator |
| Evaluator notes submitted | Within 3 business days | All Evaluators |
| Draft AAR/IP | Within 14 business days | Lead Evaluator |
| Review period | 7-10 business days | Exercise Director, key stakeholders |
| Final AAR/IP distributed | Within 45-60 calendar days | Exercise Director |
| Improvement Plan tracking begins | Upon AAR/IP distribution | Designated IP Manager |
| First progress review | 90 days post-exercise | IP Manager, Exercise Director |

### Distribution List:
- Exercise Director
- All participating department heads
- Executive sponsor
- Security leadership
- Evaluators
- Planning team members
- [Additional stakeholders as appropriate per organizational policy]

### Retention:
Maintain AAR/IPs for a minimum of 3 years to support progressive exercise planning, regulatory compliance, and trend analysis across exercise programs.
